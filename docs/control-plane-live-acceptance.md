## Slack shared-context authorization

Signed Slack events establish their team, channel, thread and author; a bot observing a thread does not grant every dashboard room owner permission to import it. Collaboration source discovery, linking, inbound writes and outbound delivery require an exact `SLACK_CANVAS_OWNERS` team/channel entry. Its `ownerId` must be the active human room owner, and every room reader must be that owner or one of the explicitly configured Access `viewerIds`. The existing strict parser rejects the entire policy for malformed or ambiguous entries; an absent or invalid policy disables these Slack paths. Administrators must configure verified owner/viewer identities and channel authority rather than infer either from a display name or guessed Slack ID.

This policy now governs collaboration imports as well as read-only run canvases. Imported Slack authors retain signed provenance and message-send-only room participation; their Slack IDs do not become dashboard identities. Membership and import writes recheck the audience in SQL. Retained links preserve the original audience restriction after unlinking, and current-policy checks protect retained context when a grant is removed. Outbound messages additionally require their existing action-bound human approval, configured destination, matching bot installation, frozen content and final pre-send authorization check.
